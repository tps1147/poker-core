# Sources for the Welcome track's history and learning-science claims

Backs the `w-history` truth sheet (H1–H13) and the `w-the-academy` truth sheet (L1–L2) in `welcome.md`. Ids `[S1]`… in the film captions point here. Checked 2026-10-06 by web research (read only). Quotes are kept under 15 words, one per source at most.

Status key: **VERIFIED** (a source supports the wording as captioned), **CORRECTED** (the planned claim was wrong or imprecise; the corrected wording is what the film uses), **UNVERIFIED** (no reliable source; the film does not state it).

## Claims

| Claim | Status | Verified wording (what the film may say) | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | VERIFIED | Poker was played on Mississippi steamboats by about 1830, and in New Orleans in 1829. | S1, S2 |
| H2 | CORRECTED | The earliest recorded American game used a 20-card deck (A K Q J T). The first-hand account is Joseph Cowell's, of New Orleans in 1829, published in 1844; Jonathan H. Green's book is from 1843. Not "Green / Cowell in the 1830s". | S1, S2, S3, S4 |
| H3 | CORRECTED | The full 52-card deck replaced the 20-card deck later; draw poker appeared about 1860. Dropped: "by the mid-1800s" as a firm date, and "stud spread around the Civil War" (no reliable source found, UNVERIFIED, not captioned). | S1, S3 |
| H4 | CORRECTED | By tradition the first hand of Texas Hold'em was dealt in Robstown, Texas, in the early 1900s; the Texas Legislature recognised this in 2007 (HCR 109). It is tradition, not documented fact, so the caption says "tradition says". | S5 |
| H5 | CORRECTED | Hold'em reached Las Vegas in the 1960s. Sources disagree on the year and place: HCR 109 says the Golden Nugget in 1967; other accounts credit Corky McCorquodale at the California Club in 1963. The film says "the 1960s" and names no one and no casino. | S5, S6 |
| H6 | VERIFIED | The World Series of Poker began in 1970 at Binion's Horseshoe in Las Vegas (Benny Binion). Johnny Moss was named champion by a vote of the players. | S5, S7 |
| H7 | VERIFIED (background, not captioned) | From 1971 the championship was a freezeout tournament, won again by Moss. Accounts give the 1971 field as six or seven players, so no field size is stated. | S7 |
| H8 | VERIFIED | Chris Moneymaker, an amateur (an accountant from Tennessee) who qualified through an online PokerStars satellite, won the 2003 WSOP Main Event. Buy-in and prize figures are not captioned. | S8 |
| H9 | CORRECTED | "Millions started playing" is not sourced. Use the WSOP Main Event field instead: 839 entrants in 2003, 8,773 in 2006, more than ten times bigger. The World Poker Tour / hole-card camera detail rests only on tertiary sources (UNVERIFIED) and is not captioned. | S8 |
| H10 | VERIFIED (optional beat, not captioned) | Polaris (University of Alberta) beat a team of pros at heads-up limit Hold'em in Las Vegas, July 2008. Cepheus "essentially solved" heads-up limit Hold'em (Science, January 2015). | S9, S10 |
| H11 | VERIFIED | Libratus (Carnegie Mellon) beat four top pros at heads-up no-limit Hold'em over 120,000 hands, January 2017 (Science, 2018). DeepStack (Alberta) beat pros at heads-up no-limit over 44,000 hands (Science, 2017). | S11, S12, S13 |
| H12 | VERIFIED | Pluribus (Carnegie Mellon and Facebook AI) beat elite pros at six-player no-limit Hold'em (Science, July 2019). | S14, S15 |
| H13 | VERIFIED | In poker the house does not play against you; it takes a fee (the rake) for running the game. The time-charge variant is not captioned. | S16 |
| L1 | VERIFIED | Spacing study sessions apart improves long-term retention over massed study (cramming). | S17 |
| L2 | VERIFIED | Practising recall (testing yourself) improves long-term retention more than restudying the same material. | S18 |
